# Breakout Maker — NOCTURNE design guidelines

## Current direction

The maintained product is NOCTURNE: a lunar brick-breaker with a centered crystalline court, a precision mechanical paddle and a compact Chinese-first interface. These guidelines describe the current edition. Earlier steampunk, three-column arcade and bright studio-table concepts are historical explorations; see the [version archive](archive/README.md).

Use current implementation and explicit user direction as the source of truth. [Visual details](web/design-notes.md) and [gameplay feedback](web/arcade-direction.md) expand this guide.

## Audience and experience

Casual mobile players, desktop arcade players and people who enjoy making levels from images or descriptions should be able to start a short session immediately. Campaign, image conversion and AI creation are equal entry points. All 13 campaign stages are open from the start.

The personality is creative, precise and approachable. A dark celestial setting supplies atmosphere; clear controls, readable text and immediate feedback make it welcoming. The court owns attention during play, while the lobby makes the three modes easy to discover.

## Visual language

- Centered, horizontally symmetrical perspective during play. Frame the full court for the viewport; keep the camera still during gameplay.
- Midnight nebulae, a shaded moon, reflective dark glass, luminous crystals and machined metal. Use local procedural assets and controlled bloom.
- A detailed paddle with a recessed energy core. The ball and collision surfaces must remain easy to distinguish from scenery.
- Full-height play layout: navigation and live notifications above the court, compact controls below it. No gameplay sidebars or text bursts across the active ball path.
- Bundled Space Grotesk for display/body Latin text, JetBrains Mono for numeric labels, and PingFang/system fallbacks for Chinese. Chinese is the primary product language; the project README also supports English.

## Interaction principles

1. **Keep the ball readable.** Effects support contact and direction; scenery and bloom must not resemble extra balls or conceal the approach to the paddle.
2. **Make movement stable.** Mouse and touch targets are smoothed in the simulation. Render the paddle at its actual collider position, with no separate follow lag, bobbing or tilt. Keyboard input remains direct.
3. **Reserve space for feedback.** Pickups, combos and effect timers stay in the header at every viewport size. Ready, pause and result prompts can appear when play is stopped.
4. **Keep creation simple.** Choose or describe an image, preview the resulting bricks, then play. Explain AI quota and personal-key options at the point where they matter. Local image conversion stays on the device.
5. **Respect authored content.** Preserve imported image colors and the original stage 13 dedication in `levels/preserved/lu-yuan-easter-egg.json`. Runtime presentation tuning belongs in the modern adapter.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

- Keep score, lives, pause and skill controls usable at narrow mobile widths; avoid horizontal overflow and protect the full playfield.
- Provide visible focus states, labels for icon controls and generous touch targets. Keep essential actions available through buttons and the documented keyboard controls.
- Pair brick colors with material/shape cues, legend labels and stage briefings.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences, retain the low-quality option and Canvas compatibility rendering, and avoid full-screen strobes.
- Music is opt-in. Mute, pause and background transitions must stop audio; pausing also freezes gameplay timers.
